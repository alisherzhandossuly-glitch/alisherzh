01 / Research Question

What are we actually asking?

How is exposure to academic achievement content on social media associated with academic self-confidence among undergraduate students at Nazarbayev University today?
Explanatory VariableExposure to academic achievement content on social media
Outcome VariableAcademic self-confidence
PopulationUndergraduate students at Nazarbayev University
02 / Epistemology

Positivist by design.

The project treats exposure and academic self-confidence as observable, measurable variables.
A positivist approach fits because the study asks whether a systematic relationship can be observed between measurable variables. It enables a clear hypothesis and an empirical test rather than relying only on interpretation.
Measure the relationship. Do not assume the effect.
03 / Contribution

Two conversations.
One missing connection.

Social media creates comparison opportunities. Academic comparison can shape confidence.
This project brings those literatures together by focusing specifically on academic-achievement content encountered on social media and its association with academic self-confidence in the context of Nazarbayev University.

The contribution is deliberately narrow: it does not claim that nobody has studied the topic before. It asks whether a known comparison process appears in a specific academic-content environment and population.
04 / Literature Review

Five studies.
Three conversations.

01

Academic comparison

Social comparisons in academic settings are linked to academic self-confidence and show how relative performance cues can matter for self-evaluation.

Pulford, Woodward & Taylor · 2018
02

Social media comparison

Social media can intensify opportunities for upward comparison, with implications for self-evaluation and self-esteem.

Vogel et al. · 2014
03

Academic self-efficacy

Research on first-year university students links social-media intensity to academic self-efficacy, connecting digital environments to academic beliefs.

Individual susceptibility

The effects of social comparison online can depend on how individuals interpret and respond to comparison information.

de Vries & Kühne · 2015

Digital comparison

Recent work continues to examine how university students respond to social comparison in digital environments.

Ding et al. · 2025
05 / Puzzle
Why can another student's success be motivating for one student, but discouraging for another?
The same achievement post can produce different self-evaluations. That tension motivates the mechanism at the center of the study.

08 / Falsifiability

What would change my mind?

A good hypothesis must be able to fail.
If greater exposure is unrelated to academic self-confidence—or if it is consistently associated with higher confidence—the proposed expectation would not be supported. That result would require revisiting the assumed mechanism.
09 / AI Transparency

AI as a cognitive partner,
not an author of conclusions.

AI helped narrow, challenge and structure the design.
I used AI to test alternative wording, challenge causal language, organize literature themes and visualize the theoretical mechanism. I retained responsibility for the final research question, source selection, theoretical logic and interpretation.
10 / Reflection

What became clearer.

The biggest improvement was separating a broad topic from a testable research design.
Working with AI helped me see that “social media affects confidence” was too broad and too causal for the design I actually have. What I still need to resolve is measurement: how best to operationalize exposure to achievement content and distinguish academic self-confidence from related concepts such as self-efficacy and self-esteem.
